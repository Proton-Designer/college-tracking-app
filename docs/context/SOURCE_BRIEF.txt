Claude
The product I would actually build
After looking at current productivity systems, behavior-change research, Claude's current developer stack,
Purdue's LMS environment, and the APIs available for health/productivity data, I would change the framing
of your idea slightly.


Do not build a giant habit tracker with AI commentary. Build a personal closed-loop operating
system.


The loop should be:


Observe → Plan → Execute → Detect deviation → Intervene → Reflect → Learn → Update the next
plan.


That distinction matters. Exist already combines automatic and manual personal data to surface behavioral
relationships; Sunsama emphasizes guided daily planning and timeboxing; Reclaim automatically schedules
flexible habits around calendar constraints; and Beeminder adds real consequences to commitments. Your
opportunity is to combine the strongest parts of these ideas with academic context, physiological context,
passive telemetry, and an AI reasoning layer. 1


The biggest mistake would be optimizing for how much data you can collect. Optimize for how many
useful decisions the system can make with the least effort from you.


A useful rule for this project is:


       Every data point should either change a decision, trigger an intervention, improve a
       prediction, or be removed.


That means I would absolutely collect WHOOP, calendars, deadlines, screen usage, morning intentions, and
evening reflections. But I would not start collecting Spotify history, every heart-rate sample, every GPS
coordinate, every browser tab, spending transactions, weather, and thirty subjective metrics simply because
APIs exist. More inputs create more maintenance burden, more privacy exposure, more spurious
correlations, and more context for Claude to process.


Your concept also aligns well with behavioral research. Implementation intentions—specific "if X occurs,
then I will do Y" plans—have repeatedly improved goal attainment, and a major meta-analysis covering 94
independent tests found a medium-to-large overall effect on goal achievement. 2 Continuous self-

The academic risk engine

This is one of the strongest features I think you're currently missing.


Every class receives a continuously changing risk score.


Not something simplistic like:


       CHEM = 7/10 risk.


Instead:



  BME 301 — HIGH ATTENTION


  Why:
  • Exam worth 22% in 9 days
  • You rate understanding 2.8/5
  • Only 1 of 4 planned study sessions completed
  • Last three study blocks started ~1.4 days later than planned
  • Current projected grade is near your minimum target
  • Thursday and Friday are already calendar-heavy

  Recommendation:
  Move one BME block to Tuesday 6:30 PM.
  Do retrieval practice, not notes review.
  Target: Chapters 4–5 problems.


The score could combine:



  risk =
  deadline proximity
  × grade weight
  × estimated difficulty
  × knowledge gap
  × unfinished work ratio

  × schedule congestion
  × historical procrastination tendency


Claude can explain the score, but normal code should calculate it.


The capacity-aware planning system

WHOOP should not simply appear on a dashboard as:


       Recovery: 43%.


WHOOP's API can expose recovery information including HRV and resting heart rate, sleep measurements
and stages, cycle/strain information, and workouts. It uses OAuth 2 and supports webhooks. 6


Use that information to alter the plan.


For example:



  Normal day:
  Target workload = 100%


  Poor sleep/recovery:
  Target workload = 75%
  Protect = mandatory deadlines + one high-value study block
  Remove = low-value admin work
  Add = earlier wind-down


But there is an important anti-confirmation-bias rule here:


Never let WHOOP become an excuse generator.


The engine should use physiology primarily to adjust how and when you work, not continuously tell you not
to work because a wearable gave you a yellow number.


I would therefore implement three daily workload levels:


Floor — minimum acceptable day.


Target — realistic successful day.


Stretch — additional tasks only when capacity exists.


That is much better than a giant daily task list where anything less than 100% feels like failure.

This is a feature almost nobody thinks to build, and I think it could become extremely valuable.

Commitment escalation

I would borrow one concept from Beeminder: optional commitments with real consequences. Beeminder
explicitly uses commitment contracts and monetary consequences as an anti-akrasia mechanism. 9


Your version should be gradual.



  Level 0 — reminder
  Level 1 — stronger notification
  Level 2 — distraction apps blocked
  Level 3 — accountability partner notified
  Level 4 — predetermined consequence


Do not begin with punishment.


Make consequences available only for very specific behaviors that you have repeatedly failed despite
weaker interventions.

Weekly experiments instead of endless correlations

You will eventually have enormous amounts of data.


A dangerous temptation will be producing statements like:


       "On days you drink coffee after 2 PM your productivity is 18% lower."


That does not mean the coffee caused it.


Your system should label observational relationships as hypotheses, then deliberately test them.


Example:



  OBSERVATION
  Late caffeine correlates with later sleep.

  EXPERIMENT
  For 7 days:
  No caffeine after 1:00 PM.

  OUTCOMES
  Sleep onset
  Sleep duration
  WHOOP recovery
  Morning energy
  MIT completion


This turns the engine into a miniature N-of-1 experimentation platform rather than a correlation
hallucination machine.

The iPhone Screen Time situation

This is feasible but considerably more complicated than WHOOP.


Apple provides Screen Time technology through FamilyControls, DeviceActivity, and ManagedSettings.
Since iOS 16, individual users—not just parents managing children—can authorize Screen Time functionality
for themselves. 12


That means a native iOS companion could potentially:



  Monitor selected applications/categories
  Detect usage thresholds
  Display usage reports
  Shield/block distracting applications


But this is not equivalent to a convenient web REST endpoint where your backend requests:



  GET /mehdi/screentime/today




                                                        15
It requires native Apple frameworks and privacy-preserving architecture. Apple developer discussions also
illustrate that developers commonly implement usage displays inside DeviceActivityReport
extensions, making this integration materially more complicated than a normal OAuth API.     13




Therefore:


Do not make iPhone Screen Time a requirement for MVP launch.


Make it the first major native feature after the core loop works.

The agent system should not actually be a swarm of agents
You mentioned a coach, motivator, psychologist, etc.


Conceptually I like multiple perspectives.


Architecturally I would not start by running eight autonomous Claude agents every night.


That increases cost, latency, duplicated reasoning, inconsistent recommendations, and the probability that
different agents tell you contradictory things.


Instead I would create one analysis pipeline with multiple lenses.


The lenses I would use

Executive Coach


Looks at whether actions match stated priorities.


       "You keep saying your most important goal is academics, but only 34% of discretionary time
       this week was allocated to coursework."


Academic Strategist


Looks at deadlines, grades, workload and course risk.


       "BME requires attention before CHEM because its exam is closer and represents more of the
       grade."


Behavior Analyst




                                                       19
Looks for triggers, repeated behavioral patterns and environment effects.


        "Phone failures happen disproportionately after difficult study blocks."


I would use this instead of a "psychologist" persona. The system should help you reflect on behavior
without pretending it is performing psychological diagnosis.


Skeptic / Adversarial Reviewer


This is important because you explicitly told me not to confirmation-bias you.


Its job is:



  What story is Mehdi telling himself?
  What evidence contradicts it?
  What alternative explanation exists?
  Is this correlation being treated as causation?
  Is the system measuring the wrong thing?


Example:


        "You're blaming low recovery for missed workouts, but three of the four misses occurred on
        normal-recovery days. Scheduling appears more explanatory."


This may become one of your best agents.


Motivator


This should be used sparingly.


Not:


        "You're amazing! Keep pushing!"


Instead:


        "Yesterday collapsed after 4 PM. You still recovered and completed the assignment before
        midnight. Today's goal is not perfection; it is starting the BME block at 3:30."


Specific evidence beats generic hype.

I would specifically not use React Native as the first solution for the Screen Time component.


Because Apple's Screen Time architecture relies on native frameworks and app extensions, a thin native
SwiftUI companion will give you much less friction than trying to hide all of those capabilities behind a
cross-platform abstraction. Apple's documented Screen Time frameworks support precisely the kind of
individual authorization and device-activity control this feature requires. 12
